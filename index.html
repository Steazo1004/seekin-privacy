GeoSnap Privacy Policy

Last updated: July 12, 2026

GeoSnap is a location-guessing photo game you play with groups of friends. This policy explains what information the app collects, why, and what control you have over it. The short version: your photos and locations are shared only with people you choose, at the precision you choose, and you can export or delete everything at any time.

What we collect

  • Account details — your email address, a username, a display name, and your birthdate. The birthdate is used only to confirm you are 13 or older and is never shown to other users.
  • Photos — pictures you take with the in-app camera. GeoSnap cannot access your photo library or upload existing photos.
  • Location — your GPS position captured at the moment you take a photo, and the map locations of guesses you place on other people's photos. GeoSnap does not track your location at any other time.
  • Game and social activity — your groups, follows, guesses, streaks, and trophies.
  • Screenshot events — if you screenshot someone's photo or reveal, the app records that you did and notifies the photo's author.
  • Push notification tokens — so we can send reveal and game notifications, if you allow notifications.
  • Crash and usage data — crash reports (via Sentry) and basic usage events such as "a post was created" or "a guess was submitted" (via PostHog), along with basic device information like phone model, OS version, and app version. Usage events are tied to a random account ID, never to your username, and never include your photos or map coordinates.
  • Reports — if you report a post or user, we store the report so we can act on it.

How your location is shared

You control the precision of every photo's location before posting: exact, neighborhood, or city. Group members and approved followers can each be given different precision, and followers never see a finer precision than group members. Nobody sees any location until the reveal, and only people who played (guessed) or were granted access by you see it at all.

To turn coordinates into place names (like "Palo Alto"), the app sends photo coordinates to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim geocoding service. Only the coordinates are sent — never your name, account, or photo.

Who else touches your data

We never sell your data or share it with advertisers. GeoSnap runs on a small set of service providers:

  • Supabase — hosts the database and photo storage (servers in the United States).
  • Expo / Apple — deliver push notifications.
  • Sentry — receives crash reports so we can fix bugs.
  • PostHog — receives the anonymous usage events described above.
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim — receives photo coordinates to look up place names.

Your controls

  • Export — download everything GeoSnap holds about you as a file, from Settings → Export my data.
  • Delete posts — deleting a photo removes it and its location permanently, right away.
  • Delete your account — from Settings. Your profile and posts become invisible to everyone immediately, and everything is permanently erased after 30 days. Signing back in within those 30 days cancels the deletion.
  • Blocking — blocking someone silently removes you from each other's app: they can't see your posts, follow you, or find you.
  • Notifications — controlled by your phone's notification settings at any time.

Security

Photos are stored privately and served only through expiring signed links. Every database read is checked server-side against your permissions — location data in particular is only ever served at the precision the author chose. Sign-in tokens are kept in your phone's secure storage.

Age

GeoSnap is for people aged 13 and over. We don't knowingly collect data from anyone under 13; if you believe a child under 13 has an account, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes

If this policy changes in a meaningful way, we'll note it here with a new "last updated" date.
